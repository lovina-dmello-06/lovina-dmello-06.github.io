CascadeAuth: Separating the Resource Knob from the Decision Boundary in Risk-Adaptive Authorization Cascades

A fast-slow fraud cascade works when compute has its own knob, and fails in three ways when it borrows the decision threshold. This talk presents results on the public ULB credit-card benchmark: escalating the most-uncertain 20% of transactions matches the second tier's fraud recall at 71.6% lower compute, and why the resource actuator in a cost-aware cascade must be a separate variable from the decision boundary.

Your LLM Stack Is a 2008 Database With Better Marketing

Why ML security is dominated by misconfiguration, not missing features. Drawing on a synthesis of 139 peer-reviewed papers on production ML, this talk shows how most real-world failures come down to basic infrastructure mistakes, like the ShadowRay incident where a default config left off exposed massive amounts of data, and why operational hygiene (secrets management, encryption, scanning) matters more than exotic defenses.
